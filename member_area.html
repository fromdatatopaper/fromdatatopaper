General · Results section
Write a Results paragraph for a scientific paper. Figure: [DESCRIBE THE FIGURE] Main observation: [WHAT THE DATA SHOWS — with specific numbers] Statistics: [n=X, p-value, test used] Control/baseline: [IF APPLICABLE] Rules: - Use past tense. Report specific numbers. - No interpretation — never "suggests" or "indicates." - One paragraph, 80–120 words.
05
Before & after examples

The exact mistakes most researchers make — and how to fix them. Each example is real.

❌ Before
"We measured enzyme activity at different temperatures and found it increased significantly. The results suggest the enzyme works better at higher temperatures. Activity was much higher at 60°C."
  • "Increased significantly" — no numbers
  • "Results suggest" — interpretation in Results
  • "Much higher" — how much exactly?
  • No statistics (n=?, p=?, test?)
✓ After
"Enzyme activity increased from 12 ± 2 U/mg at 20°C to 87 ± 5 U/mg at 60°C, a 7.3-fold increase (n=6, one-way ANOVA, p<0.001; Fig. 2A). Activity declined at 80°C to 41 ± 6 U/mg."
  • Exact numbers for every comparison
  • Full statistical context stated
  • Figure reference included
  • Zero interpretation — pure observation
❌ Before
"In this study, we measured enzyme activity. We found maximum activity at 60°C. These results are very interesting. Our findings are consistent with previous studies. The enzyme could potentially be used industrially."
  • Opens by repeating Methods
  • Repeats Results verbatim
  • "Very interesting" — not scientific language
  • No specific prior work cited; no limitations
✓ After
"Our results demonstrate thermophilic adaptation: the 7.3-fold increase exceeds the 4.1-fold range for mesophilic homologs (Smith et al., 2021). The decline at 80°C likely reflects partial denaturation. This profile supports viability for industrial bioprocessing. Our study is limited to in vitro conditions."
  • Opens by answering the main question
  • Comparison with specific numbers + citation
  • Mechanism proposed with evidence
  • Limitations stated explicitly
❌ Before
"Enzyme thermostability is important for industrial applications. We studied an enzyme and measured activity at different temperatures. The enzyme showed good activity. Our results suggest it may be useful industrially."
  • No gap stated
  • "Good activity" — not quantitative
  • "May be useful" — too hedged
  • No numbers anywhere
✓ After
"Thermostable enzymes are essential for bioprocessing, yet most hydrolases lose activity above 50°C. Here we characterize a novel esterase from T. aquaticus. Activity increased 7.3-fold to 87 ± 5 U/mg at 60°C (n=6), with a 48-hour half-life — exceeding all previously reported homologs."
  • Gap stated in sentence 1
  • Exact numbers from Results
  • Compared to prior art specifically
  • Self-contained and readable alone
❌ Before
"In this paper, we study the thermostability of a novel esterase. We use biochemical methods to understand its properties. The results may provide insights. Section 2 describes methods, Section 3 presents results."
  • "We study" — not a contribution
  • "May provide insights" — too vague
  • Paper organization summary is filler
  • No "Here we show that…"
✓ After
"Here we show that the esterase from T. aquaticus TA-7 exhibits maximum activity of 87 U/mg at 60°C — a 7.3-fold improvement over mesophilic homologs — and retains >80% activity after 48 hours, making it viable for sustained industrial bioprocessing."
  • Starts with "Here we show that…"
  • Includes key quantitative result
  • Compares to existing solutions
  • States specific practical implication
❌ Before
"The enzyme was expressed in E. coli and purified using standard chromatography. Activity assays were performed at various temperatures. Samples were incubated for a suitable time. Statistical analysis used standard software."
  • "Standard chromatography" — which?
  • "Various temperatures" — what range?
  • "Suitable time" — exact duration?
  • "Standard software" — which version?
✓ After
"Gene cloned into pET-28a; expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) (37°C, 4h, 0.5 mM IPTG). Purified by Ni-NTA (HisTrap HP) then SEC (Superdex 200). Activity: 1 mM p-nitrophenyl butyrate, 20–80°C in 10°C steps, A405 at 10 min. n=6. One-way ANOVA, Tukey, GraphPad Prism v9.5, p<0.05."
  • Every parameter has an exact value
  • Columns and manufacturers specified
  • n=6 stated; statistics fully given
  • Reproducible without contacting the authors
